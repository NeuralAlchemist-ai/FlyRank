Predicting Next-Month Content Decline for Editorial Triage

A decision-support study on the FlyRank content warehouse · anchor 2026-03-31 · ~145k pages, 28 clients

Abstract

Editorial teams can review only a fraction of a site's pages each month, and the costly mistake is reviewing pages that were fine while a valuable page quietly loses traffic. I ask which already-visible pages will decline next month and in what order a human should review them. Using the FlyRank warehouse I frame it as ranking — score each page by decline risk from history-only signals (features from January–March 2026, label from April 2026), validate on unseen clients, and compare a learned model to a transparent rule. The honest result is that the signal is weak (grouped-split AUC ≈ 0.56) and the model does not beat a three-signal rule on the pages that matter — so the deliverable is that rule, gated on reach, with reason codes and human review.

1. Introduction & problem statement

This is a real FlyRank content problem. A content/editorial team maintains thousands of pages and has limited review hours; a page that is slipping is cheaper to protect early than to rebuild after it decays. The tool built here ranks pages by their risk of losing search impressions next month, so the team reviews the riskiest, still-valuable pages first. The output is decision-support — where to look first — not a guarantee about any single page, and explicitly not a claim about Google's algorithm.

  • Unit of analysis: one (client, content item) pair at the 2026-03-31 anchor.
  • Output & action: a ranked review queue with reason codes; an editor reviews the top first.
  • Cost of a wrong call: a false alarm wastes one review; a miss lets a page decay — both cheap per page, hence triage, not automation.

2. Data

Built on the gated FlyRank internship warehouse (release v20260703), read directly with DuckDB — no dataset is downloaded into the repo, and nothing client-identifying appears anywhere.

  • Tables: fact_content_daily_performance (daily GSC/GA4 grain), joined to dim_content and dim_clients for static attributes and panel start dates (context only).
  • Windows (no overlap): features from the trailing 90 days (Jan / Feb / Mar 2026, three 30-day blocks); label from the 30 days after the anchor (April 2026). June 2026 (the _sample table) is sealed.
  • Excluded on purpose (public-safe): the April label window; any 90-day column that overlaps it; trend_pct / trend_direction and the Health Score / product flags (label- or decision-derived); pseudonymous IDs (grouping only, never features).

3. Methodology

Label. future_decline = 1 when a pair's April 2026 impressions fall below 80% of its March 2026 impressions (a ≥20% next-month drop). Base rate: 0.544.

Features (19, history-only). Log-scaled prior-window impressions / clicks / sessions and deepest-block impressions; historical CTR; a past→past momentum ratio; keyword search volume, competition, CPC; word & character counts; content age, days-since-last-update (future updates excluded), age tier; two missingness flags; and three categoricals (content type, intent, competition level).

Baseline (the rule to beat). Three audit-confirmed signals — thin CTR, not recently updated (≥31 days), losing momentum — one point each, ties broken by lowest CTR. Reason codes: thin_ctr | stale_content | losing_momentum.

Validation. GroupKFold(5) by client, out-of-fold: every page is scored by a model that never saw its client. A programmatic guard asserts no label-window / product-flag / identifier column enters the features; injecting a label-derived column sends AUC to ~1.0, confirming the harness detects leaks.

5. Limitations & honest framing

  • Weak signal, stated plainly. Grouped-split AUC ≈ 0.56 — a high rank means "worth a look," never "this will drop."
  • Observational, one anchor. Measured on the 2026-03-31 slice for eligible clients; directional until reproduced elsewhere. No intervention was run, so nothing here shows refreshing a page causes retention.
  • Scope. New pages, zero-impression pages, and pages below the reach ≥ 100 gate are out of scope.
  • Threshold artifact. The ±20% label is denominator-sensitive for tiny pages; the reach gate keeps the queue where the label is meaningful.

6. Ranked recommendations

Ship the rule, gated on reach, as a reason-coded review queue (85,410 pages; top-50 observed decline 0.66 vs 0.54 base). It is decision-support: an editor reviews the top first.

  • Archetype → action: thin_ctr → review title/snippet & intent match; stale_content → refresh facts/examples if demand remains; losing_momentum → check for a seasonal dip before acting.
  • Confidence & limits: directional; roughly half the very top are false alarms. The cleanest signal is the low-risk floor — pages with zero reason codes decline at only ~0.33, so the rule is better at "leave this alone" than "this will drop."
  • Do NOT automate: no auto-edit / unpublish / redirect from a rank; no cross-client scoreboard; a human reviews every item. Monitor base-rate drift and precision decay; retrain per monthly anchor.

7. Reproducibility

Every number traces to committed receipts (the metrics JSONs and this figure); the CSV queues stay out of git and regenerate on each run. From a fresh clone, with data access and an HF_TOKEN in a local .env or Colab Secret:

pip install -r requirements.txt
python work/scripts/run_all.py    # prepare -> baseline -> train -> evaluate/export

or run the notebooks in order (w03_data_contract → w07_action_playbook, with capstone.ipynb mirroring this paper). Seeds are fixed (SEED = 42); the scripts reproduce the notebook numbers byte-for-byte.
